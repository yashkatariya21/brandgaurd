## Competitive conclusion

The broad claim that BrandGuard uniquely checks brand consistency or compliance is **not supportable** from this review: the official pages for all four vendors describe adjacent or overlapping capabilities, with Bynder explicitly marketing checks for unverified regulatory claims and Frontify advertising AI assistance that can flag off-brand content. These vendor pages do not establish that the products are identical, that every described feature is generally available, or that any vendor lacks a particular workflow.

The defensible next step is a narrower comparison, not a uniqueness claim: test whether the intended user values (a) versioned, approved facts with expiry and affected registered-content results; (b) explicit distinction between deterministic matches and possible risks; and (c) human-reviewed tests for ambiguity in the guidelines themselves. These are product hypotheses from the local project description, not verified market gaps or customer priorities. The missing Round 1 proposal and full evaluation pack also prevent checking whether this framing matches the original source material.

## Evidence limits and follow-up

- These are vendor-authored descriptions, not a neutral feature audit, hands-on trial, customer validation, or pricing/availability check.
- Do not infer a feature is missing just because it is not mentioned in a page reviewed. Request current documentation or a product walkthrough before making a direct capability comparison.
- No public dataset or API is recommended here: none of the reviewed sources can establish an organization's private approved facts or prove demand for the proposed workflow.
- Suggested validation: run the same concrete tasks with prospective users and consenting vendor product environments—update a fact, find impacted registered items, inspect uncertainty/provenance, and identify an ambiguous guideline. Record access, setup, plans, failures, and human intervention; do not score untested capabilities.
